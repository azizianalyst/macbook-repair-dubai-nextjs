## Rules that keep the account safe

These are not style preferences. Breaking them risks filtered reviews or suspension.

- **Never offer anything for a review** — no discount, no free service, no entry into a draw.
- **Never gate.** Everyone gets the same link regardless of how the repair went. Routing an unhappy
  customer to a fix first is fine; deciding who gets the link is not.
- **Never ask for keywords** or for a specific star rating.
- **Cap at 10 a week.** A spike gets filtered and silently removed — you lose the reviews *and*
  the trust signal.
- **Never review your own business** from staff or family accounts.

Then let them go. Asking twice is where it starts feeling like pressure.

## Response templates

**Respond to every review within 24 hours.** Response rate is itself a ranking input, and an
unanswered negative review is far more damaging than the review itself.

### Positive — thank by name, name the actual device, one natural keyword
> Thank you, {{name}} — glad the {{device}} {{repair}} sorted it. That {{specific_detail}} is a
> common one on that generation, and it's a satisfying fix when the board comes back clean.
> Enjoy having it working properly again, and you know where we are if anything else comes up.
> — {{tech}}, MacBook Repair Dubai

Vary it. Ten identical replies read as automated and do nothing for trust.

### Positive but short ("Great service", no detail)
> Thanks {{name}} — appreciated. Glad we could get the {{device}} back to you quickly.
> Any trouble down the line, just message us. — {{tech}}

### Negative — own it, move offline, restate the guarantee
> {{name}}, I'm sorry — that isn't the standard we work to, and I'd like to put it right.
> Please call me on 055 741 3706 or ask for {{owner}} and I'll look at it personally. Every repair
> we do carries a written warranty, so if something we fitted hasn't held up, bring it back and
> we'll deal with it.

Never argue. Never mention the star rating. Never explain why the customer is wrong in public,
even when they are — everyone reading is judging how you handle it, not who was right.

### Negative about something you didn't do (wrong shop, unrealistic ask)
> {{name}}, I've checked our records and I can't find this repair with us — it's possible you're
> thinking of a different workshop. If we have got this wrong, please call 055 741 3706 and I'll
> look into it properly.

## Weekly routine — 15 minutes

| | |
|---|---|
| **Daily** | Any new review answered within 24 hours |
| **Every job** | Request sent ~24h after handover, max 10 that week |
| **Monday** | Count last week's new reviews, log the number |
| **Monthly** | Total, net new, rating, response rate → the tracking log |

**If you're under target by month 3**, the cause is almost always that the follow-up message
isn't being sent consistently — not that customers won't leave reviews. Check that before
changing anything else.

## On protecting a perfect score

5.0 will not survive scale, and it shouldn't. **4.8 from 400 outranks and out-converts 5.0 from
233** in every local ranking model — volume and recency carry more weight than perfection, and a
flawless score on a repair business reads as filtered to a careful reader.

So: ask everyone, every time. Do not quietly skip the customer you think might be lukewarm.
That's gating, it's against policy, and it costs you the volume that actually moves the map pack.
